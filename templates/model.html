How sentiment works

TF-IDF converts English text into unigram and bigram features. Logistic Regression learns positive/negative polarity using the Pang & Lee sentence polarity dataset. The vocabulary is fit only on training text. The bundled model runs on your server; it does not call an AI service.

Positive probability ≥ 0.60 is labelled Positive; ≤ 0.40 is Negative; intermediate values are Uncertain. This abstention band is a product choice, not a learned neutral class. Probabilities are uncalibrated model estimates.

Where it falls short

The model was trained on older English sentence snippets, not contemporary long reviews. Sarcasm, spoilers, code-switching, Hindi/Hinglish and mixed opinions can be misread. The sentence-level split may share films or authors between train and test. The reported score is not an independent benchmark of live TMDB review performance.

How recommendations work

TMDB supplies a live candidate pool from similar movies and discovery. Our code fits a TF-IDF vocabulary over seed plots and candidate plots, then ranks candidates by cosine similarity (75%), genre overlap (20%) and movie rating (5%). Personalised results use up to three liked films and favourite genres, excluding saved movies. The score is a ranking heuristic, not a probability of enjoyment.

Data and privacy

Accounts store a username and password hash. Your watchlist and genre preferences stay in the database. Review analysis text is saved only when you opt in; it can be deleted from your library. Ordinary search results are briefly cached in server memory. Movie searches and IDs are sent to TMDB; review analysis runs locally. There is no email verification or password recovery in this release.

Dataset: Pang & Lee, 2005, sentence polarity v1.0 ↗. The download and full reproducible training script are included; review texts are not redistributed in this project.
